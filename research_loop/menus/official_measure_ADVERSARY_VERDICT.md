# Adversary verdict: official interleaved speed measure (commit 6bd91de)

Reviewer: manual adversary subagent, 2026-10-10. Box: Ryzen AI 5 330, 4 cores / 8 threads (SMT), `nproc` = 8, DuckDB threads = 8. Read: `official_measure.py`, `pipeline.py` (`compile_and_run`, `run_binary_timed`, `_apply_speed_bar`, `_official_rounds`), `assemble.py::_timed_runs` and main, `decl_query_measure.py` (`duck_settings`, `_time_query`), `heavy_phase.py`. Nothing modified.

Overall: the procedure is a clear improvement (same-window, alternating, medians). I found no bug that flips a verdict by itself. It still leans slightly toward us in four ways (F1-F4), and its resolution is about +-4 percent, so a verdict inside 0.95x-1.05x is a coin flip (F7).

## Noise-floor experiment (toy, this box)
Script in scratchpad `adv/e.py` + `t.rs`: 60M int32 column, `sum(x) where x%7=3`. Ours = Rust binary, fresh process per round, load untimed, median of 9 runs, 8 threads (6.7-7.5 ms). DuckDB = in-memory table, warmed long-lived connection, median of 9 (38 ms). Full official procedure (K=5, alternating) repeated 8 times under `heavy.sh`:
ratio of medians per repetition: 5.31, 5.54, 5.54, 5.56, 5.56, 5.63, 5.67, 5.74. Spread of the final ratio: min to max 8 percent, sd 2.1 percent. Per-round values of ours vary 6.6-7.5 ms (+-6 percent). Two of 8 repetitions had a DuckDB round-0 outlier of +20 percent (47 vs 38 ms); the median absorbed it. No order effect was visible (ours-first vs duck-first rounds are indistinguishable at this size). So: with an idle box, a ~5 percent effect is NOT resolved, a 10 percent effect is resolved at about 2 sigma. TPC-H Q14 (1.06x claimed) cannot be called either way.

## Findings

### F1. Python client overhead is counted for DuckDB only (FAVOURS US, small, ms scale)
`duck_round_timer.one_round` times `con.execute(sql).fetchall()` with `perf_counter`: planning, result conversion to Python tuples and freeing are inside. Our timer is `Instant` around `run_query(...)` only; `println!`/hex of the result, and dropping `res` (it is dropped after `elapsed()`), are outside. The earlier speed-claims verdict measured the fixed Python-call cost at 0.63 ms (`SELECT 1`). At a 15 ms query that is 4 percent, the same size as the effect in dispute; for queries returning many groups the `fetchall` object conversion is larger still (and our result print is free).
Fix: record DuckDB's own engine latency too (`PRAGMA enable_profiling='json'` / `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` latency, or time `fetch_arrow_table()` / `.fetchnumpy()`), and either use the smaller of the two for the verdict or at least store both in `official_rounds`. Cheapest: also time `SELECT 1` on the same connection each round and subtract it, and say so.

### F2. memory_limit 3GB vs fully resident columns (FAVOURS US, query-size dependent)
`duck_settings` default `memory_limit` is 3GB, `threads` 8. Our binary holds every needed column fully resident in unbounded Vecs (and in narrowed, dictionary-coded form: the earlier verdict showed that layout alone is worth ~1.4x). When the touched DuckDB columns exceed the buffer pool (SEC `num` 39M rows, TPC-H SF10 lineitem) DuckDB re-reads and decompresses from the file/page cache on every execution, while ours reads RAM. The warm-up and 9 reps do not change that. This is consistent with docs/COMPARABILITY.md only if that doc states the 3GB cap; GenDB used unconstrained memory. `memory_limit_effective` is recorded at prepare, but the official record does not report whether the scanned columns fit.
Fix: in the official result, store the touched-columns size (or `duckdb_memory()` / `temp_directory` spill bytes after the rounds) and flag `reference_spilled_or_evicted=true` when DuckDB's buffer pool is below the touched size; for the headline verdict re-time DuckDB once with an unconstrained limit (or only enforce 3GB when the box is the reason) and report the lower speedup.

### F3. Alternation cancels "who goes first", not "ours always has a preamble" (FAVOURS US, probably tiny)
Each ours-round is a fresh process: it first loads GBs of column files (0.2-0.5 s, untimed), so the CPU is hot/boosted and the freshly loaded columns are the last thing streamed through L3/page tables, then the 9 timed runs. DuckDB never has a load before its 9 reps; in a duck-first round it follows our 9 runs directly, and its first execution of each round is the first after a different working set. The toy test above showed no measurable difference, and a first-run outlier (round 0) is absorbed by the median, but the asymmetry is structural. Also DuckDB gets 2 warm-ups once; ours gets none beyond the 9 timed runs (the median hides the cold ones).
Fix (cheap): give DuckDB the same preamble, e.g. one untimed execution at the start of every round, and have the binary do 1-2 untimed runs before the 9 timed ones. Neither changes the bar.

### F4. `speedup_best` and `speedup_1t` mix sources (FAVOURS US if quoted; labelling)
`latency_best_us` is `min(bests)` over 5 rounds x 9 runs (45 runs) and `speedup_best = duck_median / best`. Best-of-45 vs a median is an optimistic quote. `speedup_1t` uses `duck1_us` from prepare (5 runs, taken minutes earlier) against the new median of ours. Neither enters the verdict, but both land in the same record and in `_SPEED_KEYS`.
Fix: label them `*_not_verdict`, or compute the best for both sides, or drop `speedup_1t` from official results.

### F5. K rounds of DuckDB are not independent samples; ours are (NEUTRAL, but shrinks our apparent noise relative to DuckDB's)
One long-lived DuckDB connection: same buffer pool memory, same allocator state, same thread pool, all 5 rounds. Ours is a new process per round: new physical pages (THP luck, NUMA/cache colouring), new thread stacks. So the median of 5 ours absorbs process-level variance, while the median of 5 DuckDB rounds only reflects within-process drift; DuckDB's between-session variance (13.4 vs 19.95 ms seen earlier) is partly pinned. Not a bias in the mean, but the +-4 percent resolution above understates the true session-to-session spread of the DuckDB side.
Fix: nothing needed for the verdict; if desired, reopen the DuckDB connection for rounds 2 and 4 (costs a warm-up) so both sides sample process-level variance.

### F6. Thread / core contention (NEUTRAL, one open hole)
Box is 4 physical cores, 8 SMT threads; DuckDB `threads=8` and our parallel template spawn per core count, so both are SMT-oversubscribed the same way. DuckDB's idle pool sleeps on condvars (the Python thread blocks), so no cores are held during our runs; the toy test saw no contention. Holes: (a) neither side is pinned (`taskset`), so scheduler migrations add noise equally; (b) `heavy_phase` serialises against Verus and exports but not against the MCP host, the desktop session, or the agent harness: `ps` shows desktop apps at ~2 percent CPU each, and a browser tab or the model client spiking during a 9-run window hits both sides but ours is the shorter window (ms) so a 20 ms stall is one outlier run (median-safe) for ours yet a whole-round shift for DuckDB's 9-run median only if it lasts more than ~100 ms; (c) the lock is released between the first check run and the rounds (two separate `heavy_phase("measure")` blocks, one in `compile_and_run`, one in `_official_rounds`), so a Verus proof can start in the gap, but not during the rounds (Verus takes the lock shared, rounds take it exclusively). Acceptable.
Fix (optional): record `os.getloadavg()` and the count of other runnable processes before each round; drop/redo a round when load1 minus our own exceeds 0.5. Not needed if F7 is addressed with more rounds.

### F7. K=5 median of medians does not resolve a 5-10 percent effect (NEUTRAL; the label risk)
See noise floor: sd 2.1 percent, range 8 percent over 8 repetitions on an idle box with a bandwidth-bound toy; real queries with hash aggregation and spawn/join overhead will be noisier. A verdict of 1.05x+-0.05 is not a verdict. Failing the bar with `speedup` 0.97 and passing with 1.03 will be decided by noise, which is exactly what the change was supposed to stop.
Fix: (1) report an uncertainty band (e.g. min/max of per-round ratios, or bootstrap of the 5 pairs) in the record and mark `inconclusive` when the band straddles `speed_bar_mult`; (2) when the band straddles, run a second batch of rounds (up to K=15) before deciding; (3) keep the paper wording "faster than all-core DuckDB" only for claims where the whole band is above 1.0. Running more rounds is cheap relative to a proof.

### F8. Ambiguous labels between in-session and official (NEUTRAL, partly fixed)
Good: `measurement` is `in_session_single_vs_prepare_duck` vs `official_interleaved_medians`. Remaining ambiguity: (a) the key names `speedup`, `latency_us`, `duck_us` carry different meanings in the two modes and `normalize_harness_metrics` / the MCP text show `speedup` without the label unless `measurement` is read; the in-session pass/fail message only prefixes the rounds text on the official path; (b) the in-session branch compares one 9-run median against the 5-run median DuckDB number from prepare, which is exactly the setup shown to flip 1.06x to 0.74x, so an agent can still iterate against a number that is biased the other way; (c) a missing `sql`/`db_path`/`duck_settings` in an older `expect.json` raises ValueError and the verdict becomes FAILURE (loud, good) but the message says "re-run prepare", while the synthetic dev-bar silently stays in-session (labelled, acceptable).
Fix: make the in-session text say "in-session estimate, not the verdict" next to the speedup; prefix `speedup` as `speedup_in_session` / `speedup_official` or never show an in-session `speedup` as a pass.

### F9. Diff-level bugs and leaks (NEUTRAL; none verdict-changing)
- `duck_official_tmp` is created under the run directory (`metrics["run_dir"]`). With an owned temp dir (`decl-run-*`) only the binary is unlinked, so the temp dir, `declarative_query.rs`, `verify_full.log` and now any DuckDB spill files remain (`_release_binary` deletes only the binary). That was already true for the .rs/log; spill files with a 3GB limit can be GBs. Fix: remove `duck_official_tmp` in the `finally` after `close()`.
- DuckDB connection (up to 3GB buffer pool) is opened and warmed BEFORE round 0 and stays open while our binary loads several GB per ours-round. `heavy_phase` waits for only 3500 MB available. Under a `MemoryMax=5G` scope (heavy.sh default) this can page-cache-evict the DB file or OOM; DuckDB rounds after evictions re-read from disk (disfavours DuckDB, noise) and an OOM kills the whole host measure. Fix: set the wait threshold to `memory_limit + column files size`, or close/reopen is not needed, just check.
- `prepare` connection: `con.close()` is in `finally`, good. A second read-only connect to the same file from a process that still holds a read-write one raises (converted to ValueError, loud). Fine.
- `run_binary_timed` uses the same `cwd=directory` and no env change as the first run, so the relative column paths behave the same. `heavy_phase` leaves `LEMMA_HEAVY_LOCK_HELD=1` in `os.environ` during the rounds, inherited by the binary (harmless). It pops it unconditionally in `finally`, which is correct only because the nested case returns early.
- Check run's timing is discarded as stated; `latency_us` in the result is overwritten by the median of ours. `check_run_latency_us` is kept. Good.
- Synthetic bar: `official` is forced False, `keep_binary` is False, so no leak and the result says in-session. In the failure branch of `_apply_speed_bar` for non-SUCCESS statuses, `_release_binary` handles a kept binary. A non-owned `work_dir` keeps its binary as before (documented).
- Timed-out round: `subprocess.TimeoutExpired` from `run_binary_timed` is caught and becomes a loud FAILURE (not a fallback). Good.
- `timeout=run_timeout_sec()` applies per round invocation, so a slow binary can take 5x the old wall time inside the exclusive lock. Acceptable, but it blocks all proofs for that long.

## Verdict
HOLDS WITH CONDITIONS. The new official number is a fairer same-window comparison than before and is not biased by order. It is mildly optimistic for us by F1 (+2-5 percent at ms scale), F2 (size dependent, can be large for the 39M-row SEC tables), F3 (probably <1 percent). Do not quote an official speedup within +-10 percent of 1.0 as a win or a loss until F7's band and F1's engine-latency figure are recorded. The 1.0x bar itself is unchanged, as intended.
